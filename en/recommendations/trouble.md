  - type: 'steps'
    title: '... If your pet gets sick'
    list:
      - name: 'Assess the condition'
        description: 'Refusal to eat, lethargy, vomiting, diarrhea, or high fever are reasons to contact a vet immediately. Do not self-medicate.'
      - name: 'Contact the clinic'
        description: 'Call the nearest 24-hour clinic. Describe the symptoms so the doctor can tell you whether urgent hospitalization is needed or if you can come in for a scheduled visit.'
      - name: 'Provide rest'
        description: 'Isolate the pet from children and other animals. Place a bowl of water nearby. Do not give any medications without a doctor’s prescription.'
  - type: 'steps'
    title: '... If your pet gets lost'
    list:
      - name: 'Search near your home'
        description: 'Dogs usually don’t run far. Check the yard, entrances, and the nearest park. Cats hide within a 100-meter radius — look under cars and in basements.'
      - name: 'Put up flyers'
        description: 'Include the pet’s name, distinguishing features, area, and your phone number. Offer a reward. Post in local chats and social media.'
      - name: 'Check shelters'
        description: 'Call nearby shelters and animal control services. Send them a recent photo of the pet and a description of its features.'
  - type: 'steps'
    title: '... If your pet has passed away'
    list:
      - name: 'Give yourself time'
        description: 'Grief is normal. Don’t hide your emotions. Losing a friend is a great loss, and everyone needs time to cope.'
      - name: 'Arrange a farewell'
        description: 'You can contact a veterinary clinic for cremation or choose a burial site (in accordance with sanitary regulations). Allow yourself to say goodbye with dignity.'
      - name: 'Take care of yourself'
        description: 'Don’t stay alone with your grief. Talk to loved ones, consult a psychologist, or join support groups for pet owners.'
